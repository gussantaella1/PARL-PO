\begin{vita}

\theauthor\ will graduate in August 2026 with his Masters of Science in Engineering (MSE) degree in Aerospace Engineering, with an emphasis in Controls, Autonomy, and Robotics and a graduate certificate in Robotics, at the University of Texas at Austin. He's been a full-time graduate research assistant at the Controls Group for Distributed and Uncertain Systems since August 2025, advised by Dr. Maruthi Akella.

During his master's program, he completed two internships in guidance, navigation, and controls engineering. The first was at Blue Origin in Kent, Washington where he worked on the flight and ground autonomy team testing launch readiness protocols for New Glenn orbital launch rocket, from January 2025 to May 2025. The second was at Intuitive Machines, where he worked on the optical navigation team and tested algorithmic improvements to the terrain relative navigation (TRN) protocols for the Nova-C lander.

Before his graduate degree, Gus earned his Bachelor of Arts in Physics from the University of Colorado Boulder in May 2022. While there, he worked at JILA/NIST as an experimental physics research assistant under Eric Cornell and Jun Ye in the JILA eEDM Generation III experiment, measuring the electron's electric dipole moment in ThF+ to test the Standard Model of physics, from September 2020 until February 2023. As part of his involvement in the group, he completed a thesis on using magnetic coils for Zeeman splitting and for maintaining ambient-field homogeneity of the ThF+ ions.

After he graduates from his MSE degree, he'll start full-time employment at Blue Origin in September 2026, where he'll work on the guidance, navigation, and controls algorithm team for the Blue Moon MK1 autonomous lunar lander.




\end{vita}
