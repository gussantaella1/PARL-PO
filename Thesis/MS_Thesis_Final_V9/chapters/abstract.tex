This thesis studies reinforcement learning for adversarial orbital spacecraft planning under realistic guidance, navigation, and control constraints. For this, we propose Phased Adversarial Reinforcement Learning under Partial Observability (\acs{parlpo}) to train robust policies for a two-player, zero-sum Target-Attacker-Defender (\acs{tad}) orbital defense scenario. This is accomplished through a staged training framework in which policies are trained against progressively more capable opponents, which improves policy robustness, reduces overfitting to the training dataset, and stabilizes the learning. We extend the framework to partial observability by using bearings-only Extended Kalman-filter state estimates of the opponent with full-state reward evaluation during training, examine how learned policies interact with a maximum-velocity-limiting \acs{cbfqp} safety controller in the loop, and evaluate whether policies trained under \acs{hcw} dynamics transfer to previously unseen dynamics. Monte Carlo evaluations across policy matchups show that \acs{parlpo} produces coherent and competitive attacker and defender policies for orbital defense games.
