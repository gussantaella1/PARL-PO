Compute-wise, policies had a compute time of0.062–0.145 ms per timestep in
the full-state case and3.72–3.91 ms per timestep in the KF-on case, the latter which
the EKF update time inside the evaluation rollout loop, across all primary evaluation
runs.
Overall, we see pretty coherent and consistent results throughout these eval-
uation cases that help us prove the competency of the trained policies and their
competitiveness at the time of evaluation. We note that actuation and velocity limits
do affect the overall outcomes, with the best defender results coming from the highest
actuation settings.
From the numbers presented of these results, we obtain our figure of merit
for the abstract and conclusion: where full state policies reached up to 79.9% win
rate for the defender, dropping down to up to just 74.7% in the EKF-based case
(signaling a 6.5% relative drop from full observability to partial observability), within
the bounds of the 20 meter arena it was trained on. On the larger 100 m radius arena,
which the policies where never trained on, the full state policies reached up to 66.2%,
while the EKF-based cases struggled at a much more even 47.4% win rate (signaling
a 28.4% relative drop from going from full observability to partial observability).
