6.4 Analysis of results and anecdotes of development
The results in section ?? show that the proposed PARL+BC method for
adversarial reinforcement learning-based GNC is not only feasible but successful in
constrained zero-sum strategy games with continuous control. We attribute this
finding due to the following reasons:
The well-defined, zero-sum objective definition via the reward function and
the episodic termination conditions properly guide agents towards learning
coherent policies
The objectives that both spacecraft have to fulfill are well defined, if not over-
defined, by the reward function plus the episodic terminal conditions. The zero-sum
framing of this problem helps the agents have these defined objectives.
In fact, early on in this research we focused on stand-off games instead of
zero-sum games, where the defending agent would have to defend the asset from the
attacker without docking onto the attacker, and both agents would be penalized if
they docked/crashed into each other. This is inherently a non-zero sum game with
shared equality constrains and an open loop, standoff objective.
The result was fairly underwhelming after months of research, where we
couldn’t develop successful defender policies. Our conclusion was that you should
try to solve not solve open-loop and undefined learning objectives with reinforcement
learning. Instead, open-loop Nash equilibrium solvers and other classical optimal
control frameworks might best suit these sorts of research tasks.
